# Journal strategy

Assessment date: 7 September 2026. Scope statements below are publisher-derived; recommendations about fit and professional visibility are editorial judgments, not bibliometric rankings or guarantees of institutional credit.

## Recommendation

**Develop the completed study toward AIAA Journal, with Computers & Fluids as a strong alternative rather than a venue of last resort.** For the current Euler-only evidence, Computers & Fluids is the more natural fit. For the intended paper about remeshing effects on unsteady RANS aerodynamics, a convincing RANS application and resolved verification issues would make AIAA Journal the preferred first target for an aerospace-CFD research profile.

Do not choose a higher-status-sounding journal at the expense of the paper's evidentiary fit. A well-positioned archival contribution that reaches the relevant CFD/aerodynamics audience is more useful than extending submission cycles around an unsupported novelty claim. Actual CV evaluation depends on the institution, discipline, competition, authorship contribution, and the work's reception; no numerical career-score ranking is claimed here.

## AIAA Journal — preferred aerospace-facing target after completion

The official scope explicitly includes advances in computational and experimental methods and new understanding of aerospace fundamentals. The paper's strongest AIAA framing would be: remeshing changes the time-integration history and aerodynamic loads; global integral control alone is insufficient; and the effect is quantified on a practical unsteady RANS case after controlled Euler verification.

The mixed-history experiment and load-transient interpretation are relevant. The novelty must come from a well-controlled aerodynamic finding and a useful assessment methodology, not from deriving the known BDF2 coefficients or comparing established interpolants alone. The missing RANS evidence, physical-step convergence checks, and trustworthy extraction workflow should be completed first.

Keep the main text focused on the engineering/numerical conclusion and place complete parameter matrices in supplemental material or appendices subject to the journal's format. The official AIAA guidance describes regular articles as typically around 10,000–12,000 word-equivalents including equations and figure/table space, and asks for concision. The present 28 single-spaced pages should not be equated directly to that guidance.

Source: AIAA, “Journal Scopes and Content,” https://aiaa.org/publications/journals/journal-scopes-and-content/ (accessed 7 September 2026).

## Computers & Fluids — best fit for the comparative CFD-methods emphasis

The publisher scope includes numerical methods for fluid-flow computation, hydro/aerodynamics, turbulence, flow stability, aeroacoustics, and computational analysis of flow physics. A rigorous comparison of remeshing/transfer errors with clearly delimited mathematical analysis fits that remit directly.

This would be a credible choice for building a CFD-methods profile, not merely a fallback for an AIAA rejection. A complete comparative study, including cost and robustness, is a more natural framing here than a claim to a fundamentally new projection algorithm. If the paper remains Euler-only, its title and conclusions must remain Euler/general-CFD in scope regardless of venue, and the present numerical/reproducibility issues still need resolution.

Source: Elsevier, Computers & Fluids description, https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/computers-and-fluids/0045-7930 (accessed 7 September 2026).

## Journal of Computational Physics — ambitious, but not the best present target

The official scope emphasizes original advanced numerical/mathematical modeling and innovative methods, including comparisons, efficacy, robustness, computational complexity, and reproducibility. This is attractive for a methods-centered career profile, but the current study primarily evaluates established transfer constructions and tests a specialized consequence of classical multistep algebra.

For JCP, a stronger contribution might be a new history/output-aware transfer construction, a nontrivial error estimate under repeated remeshing, or a broadly demonstrated principle that changes method design. These are possible research directions, not improvements that can be asserted in the existing paper. I would not delay a sound AIAA/C&F submission merely to pursue JCP unless such a contribution is already emerging from the work.

Source: Elsevier, Journal of Computational Physics description, https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/journal-of-computational-physics/0021-9991 (accessed 7 September 2026).

## Journal of Aircraft — relevant only with a more aircraft-application-centered contribution

Its official scope includes applied CFD and unsteady aircraft aerodynamics. However, this manuscript is primarily about transfer operators and numerical restart error, not a new aircraft-technology finding. A NACA benchmark alone does not make Journal of Aircraft the optimal target. It could become appropriate if the main scientific outcome concerns an aircraft configuration or design consequence rather than transfer methodology.

Source: the same AIAA scope page above.

## Submission decision after the next results

A practical decision rule is:

- A strong unsteady-RANS load/phase/separation result plus the current controlled history tests: target AIAA Journal first.
- A broad, reproducible transfer-comparison paper with cost/robustness and a CFD-methods emphasis: target Computers & Fluids directly.
- A genuinely new, generalizable numerical method or theory: reassess Journal of Computational Physics.

No impact-factor, quartile, acceptance-rate, fee, or review-duration claims are used. Those are time-dependent and would need a separate, verified comparison close to submission.
