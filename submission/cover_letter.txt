To the Editors of the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics

Dear Editors,

We submit “Mesh-to-Mesh Transfer Errors in Multistep Unsteady Flow Simulations” for consideration as an original research paper in the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics.

The manuscript examines how solution-transfer errors affect unsteady flow simulations with remeshing. It compares nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, barycentric interpolation, Galerkin projection, and a control-volume transfer through static fields, Euler simulations with repeated remeshing, controlled airfoil restarts, and fixed-body Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes calculations.

The controlled comparisons distinguish preservation of spatial profiles from conservation and the response of multistep time integration. With globally corrected first-order interpolation, the cumulative mass change across transfers remains at roundoff while the density transition broadens by 18–26 reference cells; the control-volume transfer retains the integral with at most 0.07 cell of broadening. Independent perturbations of the two BDF2 histories show that an error in the older stored state changes the next-step lift even when the latest state matches the reference. An exact residual identity explains the signed contributions of the two stored states, while the computed responses expose the additional role of the target-mesh dynamics.

The control-volume construction imposes field-integral constraints on target median-dual control volumes through a Petrov–Galerkin formulation. The manuscript distinguishes these constraints from the nodal contributions to the finite-volume integral and from the properties retained after limiting and boundary treatment. This comparison of the discrete representations connects the mathematical properties of transfer with their measured consequences during flow evolution.

With two turbulence models, Galerkin projection reduces monitored defects in the turbulence-variable integrals by factors of approximately 37–247 relative to second-order nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, while the control-volume transfer keeps all monitored defects below 5×10^−12 at the stated processing stages. These clear differences in integral preservation coexist with aerodynamic differences that remain unresolved under time-step refinement, with incomplete iterative convergence for SST. The paper therefore establishes the transfer properties supported by the evidence without inferring an unsupported RANS load ranking.
