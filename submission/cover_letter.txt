To the Editors of the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics

Dear Editors,

We submit “Mesh-to-Mesh Transfer Errors in Multistep Unsteady Flow Simulations” for consideration as an original research paper in the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics.

The manuscript analyzes solution-transfer operators as restart operators for multistep time integration on replaced meshes. It gives properties of these operators that interpolation norms or global conservation alone do not reveal, and verifies them on nontrivial examples:

1. A history-forcing identity for transferred multistep histories. For the second-order backward differentiation formula (BDF2), the receiving residual at a common trial state changes exactly by −M η/Δt, with M the finite-volume mass matrix and η = 2ε^n − ε^(n−1)/2 formed from the discrepancies of the two transferred histories. The identity, stated as a proposition with proof, needs neither a linear spatial residual nor small discrepancies. It is verified to a relative error of about 10^−7 for every transfer, slip-wall vertices included; controlled restarts show that an older-history discrepancy alone produces a finite next-step response.

2. Counterexamples that separate interpolation order, integral preservation and profile preservation. Vertex restriction can give an exact quadratic projection a second-order error in the solver integral; an unlimited conservative transfer preserves the integral to roundoff while exceeding the source bounds; and under repeated Euler remeshing a globally corrected first-order transfer keeps its cumulative integral defect at roundoff while the density transition broadens by 18 to 26 reference cells.

3. A control-volume P1 transfer posed as a Petrov–Galerkin condition on the solver’s median-dual cells, with hat-function trial space and control-volume indicators as test functions. Its source field integrates to the solver’s lumped integral on every element type. The recovery matrix has the control-volume measures as row and column sums and is symmetric positive definite in two dimensions and on tetrahedra; on simplicial meshes the Jacobi-preconditioned spectrum lies in a fixed disc about one. The transfer is globally conservative; per control volume, its condition holds before limiting and away from the boundary trace. Against Galerkin projection on the same P1 space, it holds the repeated-remeshing integral at roundoff with comparable broadening of at most 0.07 cell.

4. A stage-wise assessment of conservation, which reports integral defects at stated processing stages (construction, limiting, boundary trace, admissibility conditioning) instead of inferring them from the unconstrained operator.

Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes calculations with two turbulence closures are an application that tests these criteria under turbulence transport and viscous loads. The transfer-level differences are resolved: Galerkin projection and the control-volume transfer reduce turbulence-integral defects relative to nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, the latter to below 5×10^−12. Time-step and inner-iteration controls show that the paired aerodynamic difference is not resolved at the tested settings; the manuscript reports this as a quantified limit of the comparison, not as an aerodynamic ranking.
