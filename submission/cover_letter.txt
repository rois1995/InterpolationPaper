To the Editors of the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics

Dear Editors,

We submit “Mesh-to-Mesh Transfer Errors in Multistep Unsteady Flow Simulations” for consideration as an original research paper in the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics.

The manuscript examines how solution-transfer errors affect unsteady simulations with remeshing. It compares nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, barycentric interpolation, Galerkin projection, and control-volume transfer through static fields, a Riemann problem with repeated remeshing, controlled BDF2 airfoil restarts, and fixed-body Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes calculations.

The shock-tube calculations separate changes in wave shape from changes in total mass. A global mass correction restores the integral while the contact remains smeared. Conversely, control-volume transfer preserves mass across each mesh change, yet the total mass drifts during the intervening flow steps. The profiles and mass measurements identify these effects separately.

In the controlled airfoil restarts we transfer the same flow field into either BDF2 time level or both. Across four second-order methods, transferring only the older field reverses the sign of the first-step lift change and gives about one third of the change from transferring both fields, as the linearized analysis predicts. In the nominally steady viscous airfoil case, lift and drag depart from the fixed-mesh reference even though control-volume transfer preserves mass, momentum and total energy to roundoff at each mesh change.

Together, the shock-tube and airfoil results show which measurements can identify errors introduced during remeshing and which require following the restarted flow.
