To the Editors of the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics

Dear Editors,

We submit “Mesh-to-Mesh Transfer Errors in Multistep Unsteady Flow Simulations” for consideration as an original research paper in the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics.

The manuscript follows mesh-transfer errors from their introduction to their effect on wave propagation and aerodynamic loads. Static fields separate accuracy, boundedness and integral preservation. Controlled airfoil restarts then test a linearized BDF2 response: transferring only the older state changes the next-step lift even when the latest state matches the reference, with the measured response following the history weights.

A five-method shock-tube comparison separates contact smearing, shock width and position, and signed mass drift. Global mass correction leaves the profile smeared, while limited nearest-neighbor reconstruction yields a sharp but displaced shock. For conservative control-volume transfer, a closed discrete mass budget identifies numerical boundary influx as the source of the later mass increase. This distinguishes conservation across transfer from the mass exchanged during subsequent flow evolution.

Separate airfoil tests connect remote remeshing to a delayed near-wall pressure response. In nominally steady viscous flow, three methods produce lift and drag departures of comparable magnitude, including control-volume transfer that preserves mean-flow integrals to roundoff. These departures persist with smaller time steps and decrease without disappearing on the refined mesh. The comparison measures the remeshing response against a matching fixed-mesh reference at each resolution.

The contribution is an assessment of the complete remeshing procedure: both stored histories, local wave accuracy, the discrete mass budget and the subsequent load response. The tests identify which effects can be measured at transfer and which require following the restarted flow.
