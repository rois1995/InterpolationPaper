To the Editors of the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics

Dear Editors,

We submit “Mesh-to-Mesh Transfer Errors in Multistep Unsteady Flow Simulations” for consideration as an original research paper in the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics.

The manuscript examines why accurate or globally conservative transfer alone does not ensure a faithful restart after mesh replacement. It compares nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, barycentric interpolation, Galerkin projection, and a control-volume transfer through static fields, repeated Euler remeshing, controlled airfoil restarts, and fixed-body Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes calculations.

The controlled comparisons separate spatial fidelity from integral preservation and multistep response. Globally corrected first-order interpolation retains the cumulative density integral to roundoff while broadening a transition by 18–26 reference cells; the control-volume transfer retains the integral with at most 0.07 cell of broadening. Independent perturbations of the two BDF2 histories show that an older-history discrepancy alone changes the next-step lift even when the latest history is exact. An exact residual identity explains the signed history contributions, while the computed responses expose the additional role of the receiving dynamics.

The control-volume construction imposes field-integral constraints on receiving median-dual cells through a Petrov–Galerkin formulation. The manuscript distinguishes these constraints from nodal solver contents and from the properties retained after limiting and boundary treatment. This representation-aware comparison connects the mathematical properties of transfer with their measured consequences during flow evolution.

With two turbulence closures, Galerkin projection reduces monitored turbulence-inventory defects by factors of approximately 37–247 relative to second-order nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, while the control-volume transfer keeps all monitored defects below 5×10^−12 at the stated processing stages. These clear transfer-level differences coexist with aerodynamic differences that remain unresolved under time-step refinement, with incomplete inner convergence for SST. The paper therefore establishes the transfer properties supported by the evidence without inferring an unsupported RANS load ranking.
