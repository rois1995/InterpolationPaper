To the Editors of the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics

Dear Editors,

We submit “Mesh-to-Mesh Transfer Errors in BDF2 Flow Simulations” for consideration as an original research paper in the Journal of Computational and Applied Mathematics.

The manuscript follows mesh-transfer errors from their introduction to their effect on wave propagation and aerodynamic loads. Static fields separate accuracy, boundedness and integral preservation. Controlled airfoil restarts then test a linearized BDF2 response: transferring only the older state changes the next-step lift even when the latest state matches the reference, with the measured response following the history weights.

The shock-tube tests connect mass changes at transfer to the ensuing flow evolution. For conservative control-volume transfer, the discrete mass budget attributes the mass rise to numerical influx through the open ends, while the final shock position remains close to the fixed-mesh result. Applying a global mass correction to first-order nearest-neighbor transfer leaves the profile smeared.

The airfoil tests connect an initial mesh disturbance to later pressure and load changes. In nominally steady viscous flow, the three tested methods produce comparable load departures, including control-volume transfer that preserves the mean-flow integrals. The departures persist at smaller time steps and are smaller on the tested refined mesh.

The manuscript relates the two BDF2 histories to the first-step response and explains how transfer changes, boundary exchange, and the spatial distribution of errors affect the ensuing flow. The tests provide complementary measures for assessing these effects.
