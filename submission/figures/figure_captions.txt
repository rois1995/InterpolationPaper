Figure 1. Geometry used in cross-mesh integration. (a) One target triangle crosses several source triangles. The darker polygon is its intersection with one source triangle and gives one region for integrating the Galerkin right-hand side. (b) The six triangles and quadrilaterals around vertex k each contribute a shaded piece to its median-dual control volume C_k^M. The blue boundary joins edge midpoints to cell centers. Dotted lines in one quadrilateral show the triangles used for reconstruction. Only the original mesh vertices carry solver unknowns. Both panels are illustrative mesh patches.

Figure 2. Exact vertex values can still give an inexact integral. With two segments (h=1/2), the vertex values lie on x^2. Their median-dual weighted sum equals the area under the dashed piecewise-linear field: Q_M=3/8, whereas the exact quadratic integral is 1/3. The shaded difference is 1/24=h^2/6.

Figure 3. Smooth-field transfer accuracy on structured quadrilateral (left) and unstructured mixed (right) target meshes. The vertical axis shows the relative nodal density error e_{\rho,2}^{\mathrm{node}}; h_{\mathrm{eff}} is effective spacing derived from vertex count. NN, BAR, GP, and CV denote nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, barycentric interpolation, Galerkin projection, and control-volume transfer, with suffixes giving nominal accuracy order. Gray lines show first-, second-, and third-order slopes for comparison; they are not fits to the data.

Figure 4. Density profiles after 398 transfers at t=0.399, with remeshing every two steps, s=2, and deformation realization 1. Each numerical curve is an eight-line mean. The exact Sod solution identifies the contact at x\simeq2.370 and shock at x\simeq2.699. Shading marks the left plateau interval [2.40,2.46] used by the wide-window diagnostic: contact diffusion raises this plateau for NN1 and BAR2. Shock-only widths instead use [2.55,2.85]. Locally conservative flux correction (LC) has little effect on the visible broadening; several standard and LC curves overlap.

Figure 5. Mass balance and density accuracy for remeshing every two steps with s=2. (a) Each symbol is one of three deformation realizations, offset horizontally for visibility. The absolute signed sum of standard transfer mass changes excludes CFD evolution; final drift uses the direct mass integral after the last transfer. Both are normalized by initial mass. (b) Paired changes 100(e_{\rho,1,\mathrm{LC}}^{\mathrm{FV}}/e_{\rho,1,\mathrm{std}}^{\mathrm{FV}}-1), with density L_1 error measured against the exact Sod solution. Lines are means and shaded bands span the three realizations, not confidence intervals. Samples precede transfer, except the final sample at t=0.399, which follows the last transfer. Negative values indicate lower error with LC.

Figure 6. First-step lift-coefficient difference ΔC_L after transfer to the target Euler airfoil mesh, relative to the continuation of the target-mesh reference. NN, BAR, GP, and CV denote nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, barycentric interpolation, Galerkin projection, and control-volume transfer; suffixes indicate nominal accuracy order. For each method, the same error field is imposed in both stored states or in one state alone. The opposite signs of the responses with only the older state perturbed and with both states perturbed are consistent with the BDF2 coefficients.

Figure 7. Pressure response to a single Euler remeshing event in the annulus 0.20c\le r\le0.25c, where r is distance from the airfoil wall and c is chord. (a) Shell RMS pressure differences from the fixed-mesh reference, normalized separately by each shell's maximum over the 400 post-event samples; shell half-width is 0.004c. Disturbances reach shells closer to the wall later. (b) First crossings of 50\% of each shell's maximum for GP2 and CV2, restricted to travel distances of at least 0.08c. Connecting lines guide the eye; the dotted line gives travel time at the freestream sound speed. The points describe one event per method, not independent realizations.

Figure 8. Time-step sensitivity of drag with SA over three remeshing events for deformation realization 1. Panel (a) shows the GP2 drag difference \Delta C_D from the fixed-mesh reference at the same time step. Panel (b) shows the NN2–GP2 drag difference, P_D=C_{D,\mathrm{NN2}}-C_{D,\mathrm{GP2}}. All curves use common physical sample times; the horizontal axis gives time divided by the nominal step \Delta t_0. Mesh perturbations are applied at matching physical times.
