Figure 1. Geometry used in cross-mesh integration. (a) One target triangle crosses several source triangles. The darker polygon is its intersection with one source triangle and gives one region for integrating the Galerkin right-hand side. (b) The six triangles and quadrilaterals around vertex k each contribute a shaded piece to its median-dual control volume C_k^M . The blue boundary joins edge midpoints to cell centers. Dotted lines in one quadrilateral show the triangles used for reconstruction. Only the original mesh vertices carry solver unknowns. Both panels are illustrative mesh patches.

Figure 2. Exact density before transfer in the two static tests, shown on the same scale. The smooth field varies continuously. The circular inclusion has density 3.6 kg/m3 inside and 1.2 kg/m3 outside.

Figure 3. Smooth-field transfer on structured quadrilateral (left) and mixed (right) meshes. Each marker shows the relative volume-weighted density error e_rho,2^FV from Eq. (31). The mesh becomes finer from left to right. Gray lines show first-, second- and third-order slopes.

Figure 4. Separating mass restoration from profile accuracy at t = 0.399, s = 2, sequence 1. (a) NN1 with and without bounded global correction (GC); restoring the integral leaves the contact and shock smeared. (b) BAR2, GP2 and CV2 compare nominally second-order transfers on the same mesh sequence. Each numerical profile averages eight lines of constant y. The exact and fixed-mesh curves distinguish physical wave location from the flow solver’s own broadening.

Figure 5. Shock position and width for NN2 with Barth-Jespersen limiting at t = 0.399, s = 2, sequence 1. The profile is sharper than the fixed-mesh result but shifted downstream. The exact profile identifies the physical shock location.

Figure 6. Shock broadening and mass change at t = 0.399 after 398 transfers with s = 2. The three symbols for each method are individual mesh sequences, offset horizontally. (a) Shock width relative to the fixed-mesh run, in reference cells. (b) Magnitudes of the signed sum of mass changes during transfer and of the final mass drift, both divided by the initial mass.

Figure 7. Measured CV2 mass budget for s = 2, sequence 1. (a) Relative mass drift and the contribution obtained by propagating the measured boundary flux through the BDF2 recurrence; the curves overlap. (b) Their absolute difference and the magnitude of the signed residual contribution. The final complete-budget closure difference is 1.42 × 10−13 relative to initial mass. The matching fixed-mesh control has negligible drift.

Figure 8. Initial load response to repeated remeshing. (a,b) Actual lift and drag departures from the fixed Euler mesh for the first displacement sequence; thin vertical lines mark mesh changes. (c,d) Fitted changes per 100 steps for both sequences: circles denote sequence 1 and squares sequence 2. The histories show the variation summarized by each fitted slope. The 400-step record covers only 0.047 chord flow-through times.

Figure 9. A localized remeshing disturbance and its delayed pressure response. The upper schematic uses wall distance to show the fixed wall, representative fixed sampling vertices and the displaced band 0.20c ≤ r ≤ 0.25c; it is not a mesh drawing. (a) Each GP2 pressure history is normalized by its own peak RMS departure from the fixed-mesh reference. (b) Time to half maximum versus distance from the band’s inner edge. The dotted line gives travel at the freestream sound speed; CV2 checks whether the delay depends on the transfer formulation.

Figure 10. Drag on the viscous airfoil for the first set of displacements. (a) Departures from the fixed-mesh drag at the nominal step ∆t0 over the full record. (b) GP2 departure during the first three mesh changes at three physical step sizes, each with its own fixed-mesh reference. (c) Drag difference between NN2 and GP2 at the same times. Vertical dotted lines in (b,c) mark the first sample after each mesh change.
