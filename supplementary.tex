The deformation parameter $s$ bounds streamwise displacement by the smallest incident corner-triangle altitude divided by $s$; $s=2$ permits twice the displacement of $s=4$. The wide-window position is the 50\% crossing, and apparent width is the distance between the 10\% and 90\% crossings using plateaus in $W_1=[2.40,3.00]$. \section{Boundary residuals and the cancellation test}
\subsection{First-step response ratios}

The source mesh is $A$ and the target mesh $B$. After the first BDF2 step, the state difference is $\delta=\widehat U_B-U_B$, where $\widehat U_B$ is the continuation from transferred histories and $U_B$ the target-reference continuation. Subscripts $\mathrm{both}$, $\mathrm{latest}$, and $\mathrm{older}$ identify which stored states were perturbed. All responses use the steady discrepancy $\eps^{\mathrm{st}}=\mathcal T_{AB}(U_A^{\mathrm{st}})-U_B^{\mathrm{st}}$, where the superscript $\mathrm{st}$ denotes the independently computed steady references and $\mathcal T_{AB}$ is the complete transfer including corrections. The lift difference from the target-mesh continuation is $\Delta C_L$.

States are stacked vertex by vertex. The mass matrix is $M_B=\operatorname{diag}(w_i^B)\otimes I_{n_U}$, with positive target control-volume measures $w_i^B$, $n_U$ state components, and component identity $I_{n_U}$. The symbol $\otimes$ denotes the Kronecker product and a superscript $T$ denotes transpose. The volume-weighted norm is $\|\delta\|_{M_B}=(\delta^TM_B\delta)^{1/2}$ and the Euclidean norm $\|\delta\|_2=(\delta^T\delta)^{1/2}$.

The superposition remainders are $\mathcal E_U=\delta_{\mathrm{both}}-\delta_{\mathrm{latest}}-\delta_{\mathrm{older}}$ and $\mathcal E_{C_L}=\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{both}}-\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{latest}}-\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{older}}$. The relative errors are $e_{\mathrm{sup}}^U=\|\mathcal E_U\|_{M_B}/\|\delta_{\mathrm{both}}\|_{M_B}$ and $e_{\mathrm{sup}}^{C_L}=|\mathcal E_{C_L}|/|\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{both}}|$. All reported denominators are nonzero.

\revdel{Local linearity predicts latest-only and older-only responses of $4/3$ and $-1/3$ times the both-states response.}\revadd{For the same imposed error field, the BDF2 residual coefficients have exact ratios of $4/3$ and $-1/3$ relative to perturbing both histories. The first-step state and lift inherit these ratios to first order under the smoothness and solve assumptions in the article.} The state-norm ratios are nonnegative, whereas lift ratios retain their signs.

\subsection{Slip-wall comparison}
At a common trial state, the predicted residual perturbation is $-M_B\eta^n/\Delta t$, with $\eta^n=2\eps^n-\eps^{n-1}/2$ formed from the errors in the latest and older states relative to the target-mesh reference. Here $n$ labels physical time steps, $\eps^k$ is transferred state minus target-mesh reference at level $k$, and $\Delta t$ is the physical step size. \revdel{The solver reports the negative of this residual.}\revadd{The solver prints the negative of the left-hand side of the BDF2 equation, so the predicted difference in its printed values is $+M_B\eta^n/\Delta t$.} The following comparisons measure the difference between the predicted and computed residual perturbations.

At the 370 slip-wall vertices, with both residuals evaluated at the same trial state, the wall-normal momentum mismatch is $3.4\times10^{-12}$ for GP2, $4.1\times10^{-12}$ for GP3 and CV2, and $4.5\times10^{-12}$ for NN1. The tangential mismatch is $(1.4$--$1.7)\times10^{-11}$, a fraction $(1.1$--$1.3)\times10^{-7}$ of the tangential residual perturbation of $1.25\times10^{-4}$. All four transfers return the same wall state, that of the source solution; the difference in wall-normal momentum from the target-mesh reference is $1.1\times10^{-14}$, so the normal residual perturbation is negligible and enforcement of the target slip-wall condition does not alter it. The predicted and measured perturbations both include enforcement of the target slip-wall condition.

\subsection{Residual probes in the \revdel{cancellation experiment}\revadd{cancellation test}}

In the \revdel{cancellation experiment}\revadd{cancellation test}, the history discrepancies are $\eps^n=\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/4$ and $\eps^{n-1}=\eps^{\mathrm{st}}$, giving $\eta^n=2\eps^n-\eps^{n-1}/2=0$. The initial probes use different trial states. \revdel{Let $\Phi_B$ be the constant-step BDF2 residual, with latest and older states $V,W$ and spatial residual $F_B$.}\revadd{Let $\Phi_B$ be the constant-step BDF2 residual, with latest and older states $U_B^n,U_B^{n-1}$ and spatial residual $F_B$.} The time $t^{n+1}$ is the next physical time. Let $Y$ be the reference trial state and $\Delta Y$ the trial-state increment. \revdel{If the two stored-state errors are $\eps^n,\eps^{n-1}$, then}\revadd{With $\widehat U_B^k=U_B^k+\eps^k$ at the two stored levels $k=n,n-1$, the residual difference is}
\revdel{\begin{equation*}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_B(Y;V,W)
 &=\frac{M_B}{\Delta t}\left(\tfrac32Y-2V+\tfrac12W\right)+F_B(Y,t^{n+1}),\\
 \Delta\Phi_B
 &\equiv \Phi_B(Y+\Delta Y;V+\eps^n,W+\eps^{n-1})-\Phi_B(Y;V,W)\\
 &=\frac{3M_B\Delta Y}{2\Delta t}
       +F_B(Y+\Delta Y,t^{n+1})-F_B(Y,t^{n+1})
       -\frac{M_B\eta^n}{\Delta t}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation*}}
\revadd{\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \Phi_B(Y;U_B^n,U_B^{n-1})
 &=\frac{M_B}{\Delta t}\big(\tfrac32Y-2U_B^n+\tfrac12U_B^{n-1}\big)+F_B(Y,t^{n+1}),\\
 \Delta\Phi_B
 &\equiv \Phi_B(Y+\Delta Y;\widehat U_B^n,\widehat U_B^{n-1})-\Phi_B(Y;U_B^n,U_B^{n-1})\\
 &=\frac{3M_B\Delta Y}{2\Delta t}
       +F_B(Y+\Delta Y,t^{n+1})-F_B(Y,t^{n+1})
       -\frac{M_B\eta^n}{\Delta t}.
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:unequal-trial-residual}
\end{equation}}

With $\Delta Y=\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/4$ and $\eta^n=0$, the temporal part is three quarters of the magnitude of the residual perturbation with only the older state in error. The measured ratio near 0.75 also includes a spatial-residual difference and therefore does not verify the residual identity at a common trial state. In contrast, at an identical trial state the history contribution to the residual difference is exactly $-M_B\eta^n/\Delta t$. Cancellation is temporary even for an exact first step: if the first-step state difference vanishes, the older level retains $\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/4$, producing the subsequent history combination $-\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/8$.

The computed lift differences in the cancellation test are of order $10^{-9}$, but volume-weighted state differences remain 4.3--9.0\% of the imposed discrepancy. Table~\ref{tab:naca-cancellation-app} gives both state norms. Iterative error is not independently quantified, so this test does not verify cancellation of the computed state response.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the inner-iteration limit}

For a scalar history $q(t)$, its RMS at $N_s$ common physical times $t_i$ is $\mathcal R(q)=(N_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}q(t_i)^2)^{1/2}$. Let $q_{\mathrm{nom}}$ and $q_{\mathrm{test}}$ be the histories at the nominal and modified numerical settings. Their relative difference is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{test}}(q)=
 \frac{\mathcal R(q_{\mathrm{test}}-q_{\mathrm{nom}})}
      {\mathcal R(q_{\mathrm{nom}})}.
 \label{supp:eq:control-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
The denominator is nonzero for all histories reported here. The individual responses are $\Delta C_{Q,m}=C_{Q,m}-C_{Q,\mathrm{ref}}$, where $Q$ labels a monitored coefficient, $m$ identifies the transfer method, and the reference is the fixed-mesh calculation at the same numerical setting. The difference between NN2 and GP2 is $P_Q=\Delta C_{Q,\mathrm{NN2}}-\Delta C_{Q,\mathrm{GP2}}$. In this table, $Q=L,D$ selects lift or total drag. The table reports the largest sensitivity among the individual methods and the sensitivity of their smaller difference.

\section{Separating transfer error from mesh dependence}
\label{supp:reference-decomposition}

Let $U_{\mathrm{ex}}^k=U_{\mathrm{ex}}(\boldsymbol x,t^k)$ be a common exact continuum state at physical time $t^k$, with spatial position $\boldsymbol x$. \revdel{The sampling map $\Pi_M$ represents this field at the solver vertices of mesh $M$.}\revadd{Write $U_{M,\mathrm{ex}}^k$ for the values of this exact field at the solver vertices of mesh $M$.} The independently computed discrete reference is $U_M^k$, and its discretization error is \revdel{$e_{\mathrm{disc},M}^k=U_M^k-\Pi_M U_{\mathrm{ex}}^k$}\revadd{$e_{\mathrm{disc},M}^k=U_M^k-U_{M,\mathrm{ex}}^k$}. With the complete transfer map $\mathcal T_{AB}$, the discrepancy from the target reference is
\revdel{\begin{align*}
 \eps^k={}&
 \underbrace{\mathcal T_{AB}(\Pi_AU_{\mathrm{ex}}^k)-\Pi_BU_{\mathrm{ex}}^k}
             _{\text{transfer of the exact sampled field}}\\
 &+\underbrace{\mathcal T_{AB}(U_A^k)-\mathcal T_{AB}(\Pi_AU_{\mathrm{ex}}^k)
                      -e_{\mathrm{disc},B}^k}
             _{\text{source and target discretization contributions}}.
\end{align*}}
\revadd{\begin{align}
 \eps^k={}&
 \underbrace{\mathcal T_{AB}(U_{A,\mathrm{ex}}^k)-U_{B,\mathrm{ex}}^k}
             _{\text{transfer of the exact sampled field}}\notag\\
 &+\underbrace{\mathcal T_{AB}(U_A^k)-\mathcal T_{AB}(U_{A,\mathrm{ex}}^k)
                      -e_{\mathrm{disc},B}^k}
             _{\text{source and target discretization contributions}}.
 \label{eq:defect-decomposition}
\end{align}}
For a linear transfer map, the second line reduces to $\mathcal T_{AB}e_{\mathrm{disc},A}^k-e_{\mathrm{disc},B}^k$. Limiting and admissibility corrections can make the complete map nonlinear.

For a scalar output functional $G_M$ evaluated from the state on mesh $M$, a source-to-target output difference satisfies
\begin{align}
 G_B(\widehat U_B^k)-G_A(U_A^k)
 ={}&G_B(U_B^k)-G_A(U_A^k)\notag\\
 &+G_B(\widehat U_B^k)-G_B(U_B^k).
 \label{eq:observable-decomposition}
\end{align}
Here $\widehat U_B^k=\mathcal T_{AB}(U_A^k)$. The first term measures the mesh dependence of the independently computed references; the second is the restart response on the target mesh. Neither term alone is the error relative to the exact continuum solution.

\subsection{\revadd{How the history weights change for a later load}}
\revadd{The main article gives the adjoint for the first step. Here consider a load $G_B$ at step $N\ge n+1$ on the same fixed target mesh, with no further transfers. Assume the same local linearization and converged solves as for the one-step prediction. Let $A_m=\tfrac32M_B+\Delta t\,\partial_U F_B(U_B^m,t^m)$ be the scaled step Jacobian on the reference solution at step $m$. The gradient of the final load is $g_N=\nabla_U G_B(U_B^N)$, and set $g_m=0$ for earlier steps.}

\revadd{Start with $\boldsymbol\lambda_{N+1}=\boldsymbol\lambda_{N+2}=0$. For $m=N,N-1,\ldots,n+1$, solve
\begin{equation}
 A_m^T\boldsymbol\lambda_m=g_m+2M_B\boldsymbol\lambda_{m+1}
                      -\tfrac12 M_B\boldsymbol\lambda_{m+2}.
 \label{supp:eq:later-adjoint}
\end{equation}
The two terms on the right come from the two later BDF2 steps that use state $m$. To see this, multiply each linearized step equation by $\boldsymbol\lambda_m^T$ and sum from $n+1$ to $N$. The recurrence cancels the coefficients of intermediate state changes. The remaining dependence of the load on the two initial history errors is}
\revadd{\begin{equation}
 \Delta G^N \simeq
 (2\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}-\tfrac12\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+2})^T M_B\eps^n
 -\tfrac12\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}^T M_B\eps^{n-1}.
 \label{supp:eq:later-history-weights}
\end{equation}
Here $\Delta G^N$ is the load from the transferred restart minus that from the reference continuation. For $N=n+1$, only $\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}$ remains and the expression reduces to $\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}^TM_B(2\eps^n-\eps^{n-1}/2)$. The later pressure and load histories reported in the article come from forward simulations.}

\subsection{Medium-mesh remeshing response}

The medium-mesh SA test changes both resolution and the imposed nodal disturbance. Its short remeshing interval gives RMS lift responses near $1.07\times10^{-4}$ and drag responses near $5.39\times10^{-5}$ for individual methods. Differences between methods are $8.77\times10^{-6}$ and $9.94\times10^{-7}$.
