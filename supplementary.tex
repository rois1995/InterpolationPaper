This supplement contains the full Euler remeshing comparisons and additional tests of the transferred history states supporting the article. The tables also give the convergence controls for the viscous airfoil case and a companion turbulence-model calculation. NN, BAR, GP, and CV denote nearest-neighbor Taylor interpolation, barycentric interpolation, Galerkin projection, and control-volume transfer; suffixes give nominal order of accuracy for smooth fields. GC denotes bounded global correction; LC denotes locally conservative flux correction. BDF2 denotes the second-order backward differentiation formula.

\section{Repeated remeshing in Euler simulations}
\label{supp:euler}

Table~\ref{tab:riemann-frequency-app} includes the higher-order alternatives to the main-text comparison, and Table~\ref{tab:riemann-seeds-app} reports all three realizations, including globally corrected NN1. The remeshing interval is given in physical time steps. The deformation parameter $s$ bounds streamwise displacement by the smallest incident corner-triangle altitude divided by $s$; $s=2$ permits twice the displacement of $s=4$. The wide-window position is the 50\% crossing, and apparent width is the distance between the 10\% and 90\% crossings using plateaus in $W_1=[2.40,3.00]$. At 398 transfers, contact smearing contaminates the left plateau for NN1 and BAR2, so these widths are not shock-only measures. Differences from the fixed-mesh calculation are expressed in cells of spacing $h=0.01$. For nodal density $\rho_{M,i}$ on mesh $M$, let $Q_M(\rho_M)=\sum_i w_i^M\rho_{M,i}$ with median-dual measure $w_i^M$, and write $I_\rho=Q_M(\rho_M)$. Then $\sum\Delta I_\rho/I_{\rho,0}$ is the signed sum of target-minus-source density-integral changes immediately across transfer, normalized by initial mass and excluding physical evolution. Integral cancellation leaves local distortion unconstrained, so the tables report both integral and profile errors.

\section{Boundary residuals and the cancellation test}
\subsection{First-step response ratios}

The source mesh is $A$ and the target mesh $B$. After the first BDF2 step, the state difference is $\eps^{n+1}=\widehat U_B^{n+1}-U_B^{n+1}$, with both states computed on $B$. At the two input times, $\eps^{n-1}$ and $\eps^n$ denote the differences introduced by transferring the histories. Subscripts $\mathrm{both}$, $\mathrm{latest}$, and $\mathrm{older}$ identify which stored states were perturbed. All responses use the steady discrepancy $\eps^{\mathrm{st}}=\mathcal T_{AB}(U_A^{\mathrm{st}})-U_B^{\mathrm{st}}$, where the superscript $\mathrm{st}$ denotes the independently computed steady references and $\mathcal T_{AB}$ is the complete transfer including corrections. The lift difference from the target-mesh continuation is $\Delta C_L$.

States are stacked vertex by vertex. The mass matrix is $M_B=\operatorname{diag}(w_i^B)\otimes I_{n_U}$, where $w_i^B$ is the area of the target control volume around vertex $i$ in this two-dimensional test, $n_U$ counts the state components, and $I_{n_U}$ is their identity matrix. The symbol $\otimes$ denotes the Kronecker product and a superscript $T$ denotes transpose. The first-step state difference has volume-weighted norm $\|\eps^{n+1}\|_{M_B}=[(\eps^{n+1})^TM_B\eps^{n+1}]^{1/2}$ and Euclidean norm $\|\eps^{n+1}\|_2=[(\eps^{n+1})^T\eps^{n+1}]^{1/2}$.

The superposition remainders are $\mathcal E_U=\eps_{\mathrm{both}}^{n+1}-\eps_{\mathrm{latest}}^{n+1}-\eps_{\mathrm{older}}^{n+1}$ and $\mathcal E_{C_L}=\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{both}}-\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{latest}}-\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{older}}$. The relative errors are $e_{\mathrm{sup}}^U=\|\mathcal E_U\|_{M_B}/\|\eps_{\mathrm{both}}^{n+1}\|_{M_B}$ and $e_{\mathrm{sup}}^{C_L}=|\mathcal E_{C_L}|/|\Delta C_{L,\mathrm{both}}|$. All reported denominators are nonzero.

For the same imposed error field, the BDF2 residual coefficients have exact ratios of $4/3$ and $-1/3$ relative to perturbing both histories. The first-step state and lift inherit these ratios to first order under the smoothness and solve assumptions in the article. The state-norm ratios are nonnegative, whereas lift ratios retain their signs.

\subsection{Slip-wall comparison}
When both residuals are evaluated at the same estimate of the new state $U_B^{n+1}$, the predicted residual perturbation is $-M_B\eta^n/\Delta t$, with $\eta^n=2\eps^n-\eps^{n-1}/2$ formed from the errors in the latest and older states relative to the target-mesh reference. Here $n$ labels physical time steps, $\eps^k$ is the restarted state minus the reference state on $B$ at level $k$, and $\Delta t$ is the physical step size. The solver prints the negative of the left-hand side of the BDF2 equation, so the predicted difference in its printed values is $+M_B\eta^n/\Delta t$. The comparisons use residual evaluations at a common field. They isolate the history contribution by keeping the estimate of the new state fixed, as specified by the common-trial-state simplification in the article. These diagnostics check the history forcing; Proposition 1 retains the different computed new states and their spatial residuals.

At the 370 slip-wall vertices, the wall-normal momentum mismatch is $3.4\times10^{-12}$ for GP2, $4.1\times10^{-12}$ for GP3 and CV2, and $4.5\times10^{-12}$ for NN1. The tangential mismatch is $(1.4$--$1.7)\times10^{-11}$, a fraction $(1.1$--$1.3)\times10^{-7}$ of the tangential residual perturbation of $1.25\times10^{-4}$. All four transfers return the same wall state, that of the source solution; the difference in wall-normal momentum from the target-mesh reference is $1.1\times10^{-14}$, so the normal residual perturbation is negligible and enforcement of the target slip-wall condition does not alter it. The predicted and measured perturbations both include enforcement of the target slip-wall condition.

\subsection{Residual probes in the cancellation test}

In the cancellation test, the history discrepancies are $\eps^n=\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/4$ and $\eps^{n-1}=\eps^{\mathrm{st}}$, giving $\eta^n=2\eps^n-\eps^{n-1}/2=0$. In this test the reference and restarted runs start their inner solves from different estimates of the flow at $t^{n+1}$. We record the constant-step BDF2 residual $\Phi_B$ before the first update in each run. Write $U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}$ for the reference initial guess and $\widehat U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}$ for the restarted initial guess. Their difference is $\eps_{\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}=\widehat U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}-U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}$. In the expression below, $F_B$ is the spatial residual. With $\widehat U_B^k=U_B^k+\eps^k$ at the two stored levels $k=n,n-1$, the residual difference is

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta\Phi_B
 &\equiv\Phi_B(\widehat U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1};\widehat U_B^n,\widehat U_B^{n-1})
       -\Phi_B(U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1};U_B^n,U_B^{n-1})\\
 &=\frac{M_B}{\Delta t}\big(\tfrac32\eps_{\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}-\eta^n\big)
   +F_B(\widehat U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1},t^{n+1})-F_B(U_{B,\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1},t^{n+1}).
 \end{aligned}
 \label{eq:unequal-trial-residual}
\end{equation}

With $\eps_{\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}=\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/4$ and $\eta^n=0$, the temporal part is three quarters of the magnitude of the residual perturbation with only the older state in error. The measured ratio near 0.75 also includes the change in $F_B$ between the two initial guesses. Evaluating both residuals at the same estimate would give $\eps_{\mathrm{trial}}^{n+1}=0$, cancel the spatial terms and leave exactly $-M_B\eta^n/\Delta t$. Cancellation is temporary even for an exact first step: if the first-step state difference vanishes, the older level retains $\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/4$, producing the subsequent history combination $-\eps^{\mathrm{st}}/8$.

The computed lift differences in the cancellation test are of order $10^{-9}$, but volume-weighted state differences remain 4.3--9.0\% of the imposed discrepancy. Table~\ref{tab:naca-cancellation-app} gives both state norms. Iterative error is not independently quantified, so this test does not verify cancellation of the computed state response.

\subsection{Sensitivity to the inner-iteration limit}

For a scalar history $q(t)$, its RMS at $N_s$ common physical times $t_i$ is $\mathcal R(q)=(N_s^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{N_s}q(t_i)^2)^{1/2}$. Let $q_{\mathrm{nom}}$ and $q_{\mathrm{test}}$ be the histories at the nominal and modified numerical settings. Their relative difference is
\begin{equation}
 S_{\mathrm{test}}(q)=
 \frac{\mathcal R(q_{\mathrm{test}}-q_{\mathrm{nom}})}
      {\mathcal R(q_{\mathrm{nom}})}.
 \label{supp:eq:control-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
The denominator is nonzero for all histories reported here. The individual responses are $\Delta C_{Q,m}=C_{Q,m}-C_{Q,\mathrm{ref}}$, where $Q$ labels a monitored coefficient, $m$ identifies the transfer method, and the reference is the fixed-mesh calculation at the same numerical setting. The difference between NN2 and GP2 is $P_Q=\Delta C_{Q,\mathrm{NN2}}-\Delta C_{Q,\mathrm{GP2}}$. In this table, $Q=L,D$ selects lift or total drag. The table reports the largest sensitivity among the individual methods and the sensitivity of their smaller difference.

\paragraph{Companion SST calculation}
The SST runs use the same airfoil, mesh sequences and flow conditions as the SA runs. Both stored histories include $\rho k$ and $\rho\omega$, where $k$ is turbulent kinetic energy and $\omega$ is specific dissipation rate. Across the two mesh sequences the largest relative integral changes for NN2 are 52 and 37 times those for GP2 for $\rho k$ and $\rho\omega$, respectively. The integrals were recorded before the final lower bounds were imposed on $k$ and $\omega$.

The iteration record limits the interpretation of the SST loads. With 6400 inner iterations, GP2, NN2 and CV2 still reach the limit on 28, 30 and 29 of the 38 nominal-size steps, respectively (Table~\ref{tab:inner-supp}). Raising the limit from 800 to 6400 changes the NN2--GP2 lift difference by 67.7\% of its nominal RMS (Table~\ref{tab:rans-controls-app}). Halving the physical step changes the NN2--GP2 drag difference by 110.3\%, and 56--60 of the resulting 78 steps still reach the 800-iteration limit. The SST histories thus document sensitivity to the inner solve as well as to the physical step.

\section{Flux correction in repeated Euler transfers}
\label{supp:flux-correction}

Table~\ref{tab:lc-pairs-supp} reports all 36 matched comparisons. Standard and LC cases use the same mesh trajectories. Position differences use the wide-window 50\% crossing; widths in this table instead use the shock-only interval $[2.55,2.85]$. \section{Separating transfer error from mesh dependence}
\label{supp:reference-decomposition}

At either stored level $k=n-1,n$, let $U_{M,\mathrm{ex}}^k$ be the exact state sampled at the vertices of mesh $M$. The computed reference has discretization error $e_{\mathrm{disc},M}^k=U_M^k-U_{M,\mathrm{ex}}^k$. Adding and subtracting the transfer of the exact samples, $\mathcal T_{AB}(U_{A,\mathrm{ex}}^k)$, gives the error in the transferred history

\begin{align}
 \eps^k={}&
 \underbrace{\mathcal T_{AB}(U_{A,\mathrm{ex}}^k)-U_{B,\mathrm{ex}}^k}
             _{\text{transfer of the exact sampled field}}\notag\\
 &+\underbrace{\mathcal T_{AB}(U_A^k)-\mathcal T_{AB}(U_{A,\mathrm{ex}}^k)
                      -e_{\mathrm{disc},B}^k}
             _{\text{source and target discretization contributions}}.
 \label{eq:defect-decomposition}
\end{align}
For a linear transfer map, the second line reduces to $\mathcal T_{AB}e_{\mathrm{disc},A}^k-e_{\mathrm{disc},B}^k$. Limiting and admissibility corrections can make the complete map nonlinear.

For a scalar output functional $G_M$ evaluated from the state on mesh $M$, a source-to-target output difference satisfies
\begin{align}
 G_B(\widehat U_B^k)-G_A(U_A^k)
 ={}&G_B(U_B^k)-G_A(U_A^k)\notag\\
 &+G_B(\widehat U_B^k)-G_B(U_B^k).
 \label{eq:observable-decomposition}
\end{align}
Here $\widehat U_B^k=\mathcal T_{AB}(U_A^k)$. The first term compares the two discrete mesh references. The second measures the output difference on $B$ immediately after transfer.

\subsection{How the history weights change for a later load}
Consider a load $G_B$ at step $N\ge n+1$ on the same stationary target mesh, at constant $\Delta t$ and with no further transfers. At each step $m$, define the spatial Jacobian at the reference state by $J_m=\partial_U F_B(U_B^m,t^m)$ and the scaled step Jacobian by $A_m=\tfrac32M_B+\Delta t J_m$. The state difference is $\eps^m=\widehat U_B^m-U_B^m$. Let $\mathfrak r_B^m$ and $\widehat{\mathfrak r}_B^m$ be the BDF2 residuals evaluated at each run's own state and histories when its inner solve stops. The spatial remainder is
\[
 \mathcal N_m(\eps^m)
 =F_B(\widehat U_B^m,t^m)-F_B(U_B^m,t^m)-J_m\eps^m.
\]
The full difference equation at each advanced step is therefore
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 A_m\eps^m={}&M_B(2\eps^{m-1}-\tfrac12\eps^{m-2})\\
 &+\Delta t(\widehat{\mathfrak r}_B^m-\mathfrak r_B^m)
   -\Delta t\,\mathcal N_m(\eps^m).
\end{aligned}
\label{supp:eq:later-state-balance}
\end{equation}
This is the one-step BDF2 balance from the article applied at each time, with different new states and spatial residuals retained. The state and adjoint systems must use the same boundary constraints.

For the final output, define $\Delta G^N=G_B(\widehat U_B^N)-G_B(U_B^N)$, its reference gradient $g_N=\nabla_U G_B(U_B^N)$, and the output remainder $\mathcal N_G^N=\Delta G^N-g_N^T\eps^N$. Set $g_m=0$ for earlier steps. Assuming each $A_m$ is invertible, start with $\boldsymbol\lambda_{N+1}=\boldsymbol\lambda_{N+2}=0$ and solve backwards for $m=N,N-1,\ldots,n+1$:
\begin{equation}
 A_m^T\boldsymbol\lambda_m=g_m+2M_B\boldsymbol\lambda_{m+1}
                      -\tfrac12 M_B\boldsymbol\lambda_{m+2}.
 \label{supp:eq:later-adjoint}
\end{equation}
The coefficients $2$ and $-1/2$ come from the next two BDF2 steps. Multiply Eq.~\eqref{supp:eq:later-state-balance} by $\boldsymbol\lambda_m^T$ and sum over $m=n+1,\ldots,N$. Equation~\eqref{supp:eq:later-adjoint} cancels the intermediate state differences. Using $M_B^T=M_B$, the exact final-output balance is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
 \Delta G^N={}&
 (2\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}-\tfrac12\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+2})^T M_B\eps^n
 -\tfrac12\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}^T M_B\eps^{n-1}\\
 &+\Delta t\sum_{m=n+1}^{N}\boldsymbol\lambda_m^T
      (\widehat{\mathfrak r}_B^m-\mathfrak r_B^m)\\
 &-\Delta t\sum_{m=n+1}^{N}\boldsymbol\lambda_m^T\mathcal N_m(\eps^m)
   +\mathcal N_G^N.
\end{aligned}
\label{supp:eq:later-output-balance}
\end{equation}
The first line contains the contributions of the two transferred histories. The remaining lines carry the inner-solve residual differences and the nonlinear remainders through to the final load. The adjoint weights include the spatial Jacobian at each reference new state.

For exact inner solves and small state differences on a common smooth solution branch, discarding the nonlinear remainders gives the leading history-weight formula
\begin{equation}
 \Delta G^N \simeq
 (2\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}-\tfrac12\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+2})^T M_B\eps^n
 -\tfrac12\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}^T M_B\eps^{n-1}.
 \label{supp:eq:later-history-weights}
\end{equation}
The remainders are quadratic in the state differences under the gradient and Jacobian smoothness assumptions in the article. At a fixed finite horizon their adjoint-weighted sums determine the accuracy of this approximation. Early termination of the inner solves adds the residual sum in Eq.~\eqref{supp:eq:later-output-balance}. For $N=n+1$, only $\boldsymbol\lambda_{n+1}$ remains; Eqs.~\eqref{supp:eq:later-output-balance} and \eqref{supp:eq:later-history-weights} recover the article's full one-step functional balance and its leading term, respectively. These expressions interpret the forward calculations; the reported tables do not evaluate the adjoint weights numerically.
